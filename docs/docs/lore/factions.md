# Factions

Two armed factions, the UFLL and the APR, fight over the country. Each holds ground in both regions
the game is set in: Leboa-Sako in the north (Act 1) and Bowa-Seko in the south (Act 2). Around them
work foreign mercenaries, private military companies, arms dealers, foreign companies, and one
civilian network, the Underground. Citations follow [Sources and citations](./sources.md). In the
world data the UFLL's soldiers are the red faction and the APR's the blue; see
[Set dressing](./set-dressing/reading-the-world.md#red-is-the-ufll-blue-is-the-apr).

## Names

**The game states**

- The UFLL calls itself "the United Front", and everyone shortens that to "the Front". Gakumba:
  "This is the United Front. I'm Doctor Gakumba." (`4888993`, Gakumba). Kankaras: "Local boss for the
  United Front" (`4547658`, Kankaras).
- Neither acronym is ever spelled out. Kouassi mocks the other side's name: "The U-F-blah-blah is
  getting a lot of medicine from next door" (`4559279`, Kouassi).
- `CharacterNames/UFLL` and `CharacterNames/APR` hold only the acronyms.

**Cross-check.** Wikipedia gives "United Front for Liberation and Labour" and "Alliance for Popular
Resistance". Fan wikis add an origin story: the UFLL as the remains of an "Authenticity Party", and
Mbantuwe as a former labour-union leader. None of these words appears anywhere in the game's
English string table.

## Who leads whom

| | UFLL | APR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leboa-Sako boss | Dr. Leon Gakumba, at "the abandoned bus station" (`Objectives/A1LM00_03`) | Prosper Kouassi, at "the former bordello" (`Objectives/A1LM00_02`) |
| Leboa-Sako lieutenants | Joaquin Carbonell (fishing camp), Anto Kankaras (slaughterhouse) | Arturo Quiepo (cock-fighting arena), Walton Purefoy (lumber camp) |
| Bowa-Seko warlord | Addi Mbantuwe, at the Seko Brewery | Major Oliver Tambossa, at the Dogon Sediko |
| Bowa-Seko lieutenant | Hector Voorhees | Nick Greaves |

**The game states**

- The lieutenants' camps are the ones the player is sent to rob in the opening. The robbed
  lieutenant later names what he lost: "the guy who's cockfighting venue you trashed for that mafiya
  dirtbag Kankaras" (`5121241`, Quiepo). The same lines exist for the lumber camp (`5123505`, Purefoy),
  the slaughterhouse (`5123436`, Kankaras) and the fishing pier (`5122902`, Carbonell).
- The two sides describe their chain differently. The UFLL lieutenants work *for* Gakumba: "I work
  for a guy named Gakumba" (`4547599`, Carbonell). The APR lieutenants work *with* Kouassi, "a local
  guy named Kouassi. Runs muscle for the APR" (`4547806`, Quiepo); "Street muscle for the APR"
  (`4547737`, Purefoy).
- Kouassi answers to Tambossa: "The big man - Major Tambossa - said so himself" (`4558669`,
  Kouassi). Orders reach him by phone: "Boss called. Said, 'kill constable.'" (`4557784`, Kouassi).
- Gakumba answers to a "party leader": "The UFLL is almost unstoppable here - Even the party leader
  doesn't realize." (`4558591`, Gakumba). At his rally he credits "Gakumba - and Mbantuwe!"
  (`4559066`, Gakumba).
- Tambossa uses two titles: "I am Tambossa. National leader of the APR." (`4889114`, Tambossa) and
  "I am Major Tambossa." (`4889128`, Tambossa).
- In Bowa-Seko each warlord has his lieutenant at his side. Tambossa says "Carry on, Greaves."
  (`4889167`, Tambossa), and Mbantuwe says "Go ahead, Hector." (`4889241`, Mbantuwe). Both
  warlords' headquarters are in Port Selao: "as long as I'm here in Port Selao at least they can keep an eye on me"
  (`Objectives/A2LM00_01`); "I'll move back into the HQ in the Port"
  (`4561603`, Voorhees).
- Gakumba insists on the title "Doctor": "You'll call me Doctor Gakumba." (`4971256`, Gakumba).

**Implied**

- The "party leader" Gakumba answers to is Mbantuwe. Nothing names him in that line, but the rally
  pairs the two. Read the same way, Leboa-Sako is the subordinate front of a war whose top men sit
  in Bowa-Seko.
- Most of both factions' field commanders are foreigners:
  - Carbonell is "the Spaniard" (`5123505`, Purefoy) and speaks Spanish.
  - Kankaras is "mafiya" (`5121241`, Quiepo) and swears in Serbo-Croatian (`4939787`, Kankaras).
  - Purefoy was in the Navy and Quiepo in the Marines (`5180307`, Purefoy; `5180594`, Quiepo).
  - Greaves is called "limey" and "a glorified airline pilot" (`5180594`; `5180307`).
  - Voorhees spent "ten years leading most of those boys in Angola" (`4560354`, Voorhees).

  So both factions' rhetoric is "Africa for Africans" while their wars are run by hired outsiders.

## What each faction says it stands for

**The game states**

- **UFLL.**
  - "This is OUR struggle. Africa is for Africans. We do not need outside involvement!" (`4558255`,
    Gakumba).
  - "The effort to build a stronger Africa" (`4559406`, Gakumba).
  - After taking Leboa-Sako: "WE - have brought peace to this country! Now we begin a time of milk and
    honey! A chicken in every pot!" (`4559066`, Gakumba).
  - "THIS is when we take control of this country, and bring Order!!!! Stability!!! Strength!!!"
    (`4968274`, Gakumba).
  - Mbantuwe: "This is my house, my country, my continent! You are a scavenger and a trespasser!"
    (`4909607`, Mbantuwe).
- **UFLL radio.** Its station, Liberation Radio, calls the APR "a plague" and "the snakes and the
  crocodiles of the APR" (`5160431`; `4910612`, DJ Lord Haw-Haw). It sends listeners after APR men:
  "The APR snakes have entered the Lake Road preserve, south of the Airport… Take off their heads"
  (`4559557`).
- **APR.**
  - "APR is the greatest!" (`4557821`, Kouassi). "One faction! One leader!" (`4973224`, Kouassi).
  - "This is a very rich country. And soon it will be - our country!" (`4558702`, Kouassi).
  - At his victory rally: "We are an independent - AFRICAN - country! We are free men!" (`4889047`,
    Kouassi). "Get ready to take what is yours! The money! The women! ALL of it!" (`4972769`,
    Kouassi).
  - Tambossa: "This will UNITE us. One Africa." (`4559819`, Tambossa). "It is for the greater good.
    For a stronger Africa." (`4559957`, Tambossa).
- **Both.** Each side's victory speech turns on the civilians who stayed out of the fighting:
  - "If you did not fight with us, you are not with us!" (`4889042`, Kouassi).
  - "those spineless cowards that hide in their homes… THEY are the enemy" (`4971042`, Gakumba).

**Implied**

- Neither faction states a program beyond nationalism, order and plunder. The buddies say so outright
  ("These guys don't even know why they're fighting anymore. If they ever even HAD a reason.",
  `4903004`, Paul Ferenc), and so does the tape "No Sense" (`JackalTapes/A2JT15_DESC`: "Where's the
  ideology? What are they dying for?").

## How the factions operate

**The game states**

- **They pay in diamonds.** "The only currency worth anything is diamonds. Forget the paper money…
  When you work for the APR – you get paid in stones." (`4921591`, Quiepo). The UFLL says the same
  (`4921493`, Kankaras).
- **They hire deniable outsiders.**
  - "We cannot send our men on this job. It must be an outside party." (`4558513`, Gakumba).
  - "Of course we would never be involved in such a thing. It must be the work of a free agent."
    (`4559912`, Tambossa).
- **They take foreign hostages.** Both hold foreigners in "bricked-up" buildings, not sure who they
  are, "But if the APR has him, he might be useful" (`4912235`, guard; `Objectives/A1BU01_01_UBM_01`).
- **The UFLL's money.**
  - It occupies the PetroSehel fuel terminal and moves its fuel reserve in disguised tankers on
    routes that change daily (`4933270`, Paul Ferenc; `4560459`, Marty Alencar).
  - It holds natural gas the neighbouring country needs, "blackmailing our friends to the west for
    medical supplies" (`4973169`, Greaves; `4559284`, Kouassi).
  - Mbantuwe pumps fresh water out of Lake Segolo and sells it over the border "in exchange for large
    cash payments" (`4559872`, Greaves; `Objectives/A2LM08_01`).
- **The UFLL's mine.** It runs the TaeMoCo diamond mine, which every buddy who worked there calls a
  sham: "TaeMoCo is a sham. There are no diamonds. Mbantuwe tells the soldiers they will be rich… if
  they just stay with him." (`4933814`, Nasreen Davar; `4887838`, Andre Hyppolite).
- **The UFLL's image.** It has a Propaganda Minister who censors Liberation Radio on a 10-second
  delay (`4887758`, Andre Hyppolite). It brought in "this European health official for a goodwill
  tour" (`4932466`, Marty Alencar).
- **The APR's money and supplies.**
  - It has bribed the Leboa-Sako police chief "over the years", and he keeps a ledger
    (`Objectives/A1LM01_03`; `4932718`, Flora Guillen).
  - It guards a European agribusiness's greenhouses in exchange for food: "Bloody fighters over
    there are eating like kings these days." (`4559446`, Voorhees).
  - It guards the returned King's gold (`Objectives/A1LM06_01`).
- **The APR's medicine.** It holds the region's malaria remedy: artemisia dried in kilns at the Dogon
  cliff village and kept in a secret store (`4560641`, Mbantuwe; `Objectives/A2LM10_01`).
- **The APR's weapons.**
  - The gun-runner Yabek sells "exclusively to the APR" (`Objectives/A2LM11_01`).
  - The shop teacher Seth Uniya teaches it to build roadside bombs (`4561015`, Mbantuwe).
- **The APR and aid.**
  - Its bomb instructor, Seth Uniya, diverts air-dropped relief through a bribed pilot who navigates
    by cell towers, and sells it: "the teacher sells it at a profit." (`4932314`, Marty Alencar;
    `4934074`, Hakim Echebbi; `Objectives/A2LM12_03`).
  - In Leboa-Sako, Kouassi says of aid: "it has to come to US first. We know how to handle it."
    (`4559227`, Kouassi).
  - He means to cut off anaesthetic: "When we shoot our enemies, we want them to die in the street."
    (`4560079`, Tambossa).
- **Partisan accusations.** Michele Dachss says "the UFLL kidnaps children" and sells boys
  (`4933654`), and that the APR sells girls (`4887076`). Paul Ferenc says the APR uses a defoliant
  called "Agent Yellow" (`4906620`). Nobody else confirms either claim.

## How the war is fought

**The game states**

- **A long, tolerated stalemate.** Both northern bosses describe it the same way:
  - "This conflict with the APR. It has been going on for a long time. - Both sides tolerate the
    conflict, because we understand it." (`4558579`, Gakumba).
  - "Both sides are used to it! Everybody understands it!" (`4558660`, Kouassi).
- **Cease-fires that don't hold.**
  - The game opens on a broken one: "There was supposed to be cease-fire, but that's all out the window
    now." (`4547658`, Kankaras).
  - The fall of Leboa-Sako "brought a cease-fire in the south" (`Objectives/A2SM06_APR_02`).
  - Port Selao is a cease-fire zone where nobody may shoot (`Tutorial/TU42_MESSAGE`,
    `DZMessages/DZ_ESCAPE`).
- **A balance the player tips.** Both bosses blame the player for upsetting it: "things have
  changed. You have changed them." (`4558591`, Gakumba); "stronger now than ever before! - Thanks to
  YOU" (`4558676`, Kouassi).

**Implied**

- The lieutenants' interest is in war, not victory. Greaves and Voorhees each fear the southern
  warlords making peace ("blokes like you and I are out of a job. Or worse", `4559170`, Greaves;
  `4560365`, Voorhees). Each then sails the Jackal's weapons into Port Selao under the *other*
  side's flag to restart the fighting (`4559182`, Greaves; `4560386`, Voorhees;
  `Objectives/A2SM06_APR_02`).

## How the factions end

**The game states**

The full sequence is on the [Timeline](./timeline.md). In outline:

1. **Leboa-Sako falls.** One Leboa-Sako boss sends the player to kill the other at Goka Falls, then
   turns on him (`Objectives/A1SM02_APR_02`). "Leboa-Sako fell to the APR, and me with it" — or to
   the UFLL, by the player's choice (`Objectives/A2SM04_APR_01`).
2. **The losing faction regroups in the south** under Greaves or Voorhees. It sends the player back
   to kill the winning boss at his victory rally (`Objectives/A2SM05_APR_01`).
3. **The warlords die.** Tambossa and Mbantuwe agree a peace (`4560243`, Tambossa; `4561167`,
   Mbantuwe). Their lieutenants are against it: "This is the worst idea I've heard in a long time."
   (`4560193`, Greaves); "when the big dogs join forces, they're usually doing it to take out the REST
   of the pack." (`4561108`, Voorhees). The Jackal kills the warlord who receives the player's peace
   letter (`Objectives/A2SM09_APR_01`). The dead warlord's lieutenant then takes over his faction and
   hires the player to kill the other: "So here's what it's come to. Me, in charge of the whole
   operation." (`4561651`, Greaves); "I need a man on the other side of the table that I can do
   business with." (`4561570`, Voorhees) (`Objectives/A3SM11_APR_02`; `master_world2.world2:f_204_A_eq_B`).
4. **The Act 1 lieutenants turn on their own leadership**, each faction in turn
   (`Objectives/A3SM12_APR_02`, `Objectives/A3SM13_APR_02`). Each purge leaves one of a faction's three
   men alive, and he leads it to the end (`common_customboxes.setfinalwarlord`):

   | | Its three men | Who leads at the end |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | APR | Greaves, Purefoy, Quiepo | whichever one the player leaves alive |
   | UFLL | Voorhees, Carbonell, Kankaras | whichever one the player leaves alive |

   Which purge hits which faction, and who survives which choice, is on
   [Act 3](./missions/act-3.md#who-is-who-in-act-3).
5. **The factions unite.** "The APR and UFLL have called a truce. Unity at last, eh?" (`4911264`,
   Reuben). Their first act together is to stop the civilian exodus (`Objectives/A3SM14_02`). Their two
   leaders plan the hunt from one camp: "Intel taken off those stragglers near the old crossing puts
   the slower group here… No word back from the team at the prison." (`4972219`, Greaves). The
   player kills both (`Objectives/A3SM15_01_BBM_01`).
6. **The closing text.** "The remaining leadership of the APR and the UFLL united to form an interim
   government in an effort to impose order on the fractured country. They failed. Anarchy rules to
   this day." (`Coda/Para2`).

**Implied**

- Which faction wins each act is decided by the engine, not by any mission script
  (`GetWinningFactionPerAct`; see [How the campaign runs](./missions/overview.md#which-variant-you-get)).
  The strings are templates (`WINNING_FACTION_BOSS`, `LOSING_FACTION_HELPER_A1`, …) filled in from it.
  By the end, every named leader of both factions is dead, whichever choices were made.
- Before the end the two sides already behave alike. The APR men at Sepoko puzzle over villagers who
  are "Not hiding. Just gone." (`4944068`, Purefoy), while the UFLL men strip the TaeMoCo mine of
  bulldozers and steamshovels for a barge (`4939814`, `A3SMUU_SE02C`).

## Other organised groups

**The game states**

- **The Underground.**
  - A civilian network around Father Maliya's church in Pala and Doctor Obua's clinic in Port Selao.
  - It hands out malaria medicine to whoever carries travel papers and passports to refugee
    families: "people are desperate to get out of the country" (`5122787`, Underground operative;
    `Objectives/A1GM01_01`).
  - The closing text credits it with the "surprisingly small number of civilian casualties"
    (`Coda/Para1`).
- **Bastion UK.**
  - A private military company the UFLL paid: "The money brought Bastion to Africa. No more money,
    no more PMC." (`4559398`, Gakumba).
  - It "packed up and left the country" in Act 2, leaving Voorhees "high and dry" (`4560354`;
    `4560755`, Voorhees).
- **Macgrudder-Powell.** A PMC that fired Marty Alencar (`4891036`, Marty Alencar). Warren Clyde calls
  it a "clown-school outfit" (`4580015`). By Act 2 it "couldn't cut it here" (`4885124`, Marty
  Alencar).
- **A foreign Special Forces team.**
  - It is "in-country" in Act 1 to snatch "some 'person of interest'" (`Objectives/A1LM04_01`;
    `4933426`, Quarbani Singh).
  - Its radio liaison is a Belgian at a villa who says he is a fixer for foreign media (`4932366`,
    Marty Alencar).
  - Who sent it changes with the buddy telling the story: an NGO, a client, Israel.
- **Arms dealers.**
  - The Jackal, who sold to both factions.
  - Yabek, a Jordanian on a barge (`4560855`, Voorhees).
  - The two weapon-shop vendors, whose rivals include "This Ukrainian" (`4913015`, arms merchant) and
    competitors "from Moldova" (`4913179`, arms merchant).
- **Foreign companies.**

  | Company | What it does | Source |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | OGC, Occidental Growers Company | greenhouses guarded by the APR | map `w1_c_4`; `4932438`, Marty Alencar |
  | PetroSahel | fuel terminal held by the UFLL. The map prints "PetroSahel"; every subtitle says "PetroSehel" | map `w1_b_2`; `4580416`, Josip Idromeno |
  | Claes Products (Chemical Corporation) | the chemical dump | map `w1_d_3` only |
  | TaeMoCo | the diamond mine | `Objectives/A2LM08_01` |
  | Mertens-Segolo Pipeline Company | the water pipeline | map `w2_d_3` only |
  | Afreqa Telecom | an office at Sepoko | `Objectives/A2LM12_03_UAT_01` |
  | Bowaseko Brewing Co. | the "Seko Brewery", Mbantuwe's base | map `w2_d_3`; `Objectives/A2BU06_01` |
- **The international side.** The player's orders cite "the Joint Signatory Framework", which the
  Jackal's "recent influx of weapons" violates (`4577324`, read aloud by the Jackal). "There was an
  arms embargo in place" (`LoadScreens/ChSyn_AA`).

**Implied**

- The player's employer has some standing under the Joint Signatory Framework, a government or an
  agency acting for one. The orders' wording is bureaucratic, and a load screen hints at "the
  black-bag crowd" who fear the Jackal for "some history there they can't afford to have walking
  around" (`LoadScreens/MiSyn_AA`). No string names the employer.
- The Underground's internal name is "GRIN". Its characters are tagged `GRIN_Doctor_Obua` and
  `GRIN_Father_Maliya`, and its map locations `GRINLocation` (see
  [Bark banks](../file-formats/bark-banks.md#characters)). What GRIN stands for is not in the files.
