# Recurring themes

The subjects the game returns to across factions, missions and characters. Each section gathers
what the strings state, then what they imply when read together. Citations follow
[Sources and citations](./sources.md).

## The arms trade

**The game states**

- **The Jackal arms both sides.** "He has been selling guns to both the UFLL and the APR. Every gun,
  every bullet, and every corpse you have seen can be traced directly back to him."
  (`Tutorial/TU67B_MESSAGE`). His guns are cheap on purpose: "Guy could have made way more money if
  he wanted to." (`4557672`, Kouassi).
- **His tapes.** As their descriptions summarise them:
  - "Taking advantage of cease-fires, the Jackal circulates old weapons to fresh conflicts elsewhere."
    (`JackalTapes/A1JT02_DESC`).
  - "No moral code. Jackal equates illegal arms with portable radios. Everything reduced to
    economics." (`JackalTapes/A1JT03_DESC`).
  - "No ideology in Jackal's arms trade. Sells weapons for maximum profit." (`JackalTapes/A1JT07_DESC`).
  - "Jackal challenges the West's moral high ground. AK-47 the great equalizer." (`JackalTapes/A2JT10_DESC`).
  - "Jackal untouchable? Believes his role is encouraged by foreign governments." (`JackalTapes/A2JT11_DESC`).
- **A crowded market.**
  - The player's own arms vendors fight rivals by having their convoys destroyed: "This Ukrainian's
    trying to move in on my business… Bad product, none of it clean." (`4913015`, arms merchant);
    competitors "from Moldova" (`Objectives/A2CV06_01`).
  - Yabek sells only to the APR (`Objectives/A2LM11_01`).
  - Marty Alencar sells to anyone (`4907502`, Marty Alencar).
  - Nasreen Davar resents all of them: "He's a vulture. They all are." (`4907932`, Nasreen Davar).
- **Old ordnance.** Old weapons are dug up and resold: a Soviet Hind wreck "FULL of ordnance"
  (`4932346`, Marty Alencar); Yabek "has a nose for Cold War -era relics" (`4933846`, Nasreen Davar).
- **The law.** Arms were under "an arms embargo" (`LoadScreens/ChSyn_AA`). The Jackal's shipments
  violated "the Joint Signatory Framework" (`4577324`, Jackal).
- **The player's own guns.** He buys his weapons with diamonds from a shop whose stock the convoy jobs
  unlock (`Tutorial/TU57_MESSAGE`, `TU70_MESSAGE`).

**Implied**

- The player is part of the trade he was sent to stop. His employer pays him to kill an arms dealer,
  while he buys from an arms dealer and wrecks that dealer's competitors' convoys.
- The finale makes the same point: the vendor's diamonds buy the refugees' way out (`4967869`,
  Jackal).

## Diamonds

**The game states**

- **They are the only money.** "The only currency worth anything is diamonds. Forget the paper money.
  I wouldn't wrap a fish with it." (`4921591`, Quiepo). Jobs pay in "Stones. Uncut." (`4560718`,
  Voorhees). Phone contracts pay "in rough diamonds" (`5175655`, phone voice?).
- **They lie around the land.** Briefcases of them are hidden across the country, 221 in all
  (`Diamonds/DiamondPickedSwoosh`).
- **The mine is empty.** The TaeMoCo diamond mine has "no diamonds in there. Never have been."
  (`4887838`, Andre Hyppolite). It is kept running so "the soldiers… will be rich… if they just stay
  with him" (`4933814`, Nasreen Davar).
- **Diamonds carry every deal.**
  - The warlords seal their peace with "a diamond attaché case" (`4560267`, Tambossa).
  - The buddies need diamonds to pay a pilot: "No diamonds, no passengers." (`4899075`, Nasreen
    Davar).
  - The arms vendor flees with "a briefcase, full of them. Worth millions." (`4911274`, Reuben).
  - The Jackal claims that briefcase: "They're MY diamonds. Mine. I need them for the border troops."
    (`4967869`, Jackal).
- **The war has wasted them.** Reuben counts "the diamonds" among what it destroyed: "the country is
  destroyed. The people, the diamonds, the cities -" (`4577437`, Reuben).

**Implied**

- Diamonds stand where a state should be. With no working currency, no bank and no rule of law, every
  loyalty in the game — soldier, mercenary, border guard — is bought in stones. The one mine
  that should produce them is a lie told to keep soldiers fighting.

## Malaria and medicine

**The game states**

- **The player has it from the start and never loses it.** "Symptoms are gone. Wish I could say the
  same for the malaria. It's in me, just keeping quiet right now." (`PauseMenu/REPUTATION_SICKNESS_DESCRIPTION_0`). Others say it too:
  - "Malaria never really goes away." (`4902800`, Michele Dachss).
  - "Everybody gets it eventually." (`4900898`, Marty Alencar).
  - "No man's immune to the malaria. No matter where you come from." (`4939207`, Doctor Obua).
- **It is everywhere.** "Malaria situation's out of control… Everybody's getting sick these days. It's
  a bloody plague." (`4560636`, `4560653`, Voorhees).
- **Medicine is a weapon and a currency.**
  - The APR controls the artemisia remedy (`4560641`, Mbantuwe), and the UFLL hires the player to
    burn it (`Objectives/A2LM10_01`).
  - The UFLL gets medicine "from next door" by blackmail (`4559279`, Kouassi).
  - Tambossa would cut off anaesthetic (`4560074`, Tambossa), and morphine "just can't be found"
    (`4560062`, Greaves).
  - "The factions keep all the medicine for themselves." (`4932806`, Flora Guillen).
- **Medicine is stolen.** Buddies steal NGO medicine and sell it on or give it away (`4932802`, Flora
  Guillen; `4933486`, Quarbani Singh). Warren Clyde sells his to the APR "at 100% markup" (`4933034`,
  Warren Clyde).
- **The Underground's trade.** It gives pills for help: "If I want regular access to meds, I'll need
  to step up from time to time and help his people." (`LoadScreens/MiSyn_A1GM00`). A refugee
  family shares its own: "You have the fever. My daughter too. We will share our medicine."
  (`4890043`, Underground refugee).

**Implied**

- Malaria ties the player to the civilians. The only steady supply of pills runs through the
  Underground, so to stay alive he must carry the papers that get people out of the country. The one
  kindness the game requires of him is bought with his own illness.
- The Jackal's "terminal case. Same as me" (`4973240`, Jackal) closes the loop: the player's
  disease and the Jackal's "cancer" metaphor for the war are one image.

## Cease-fires and peace

**The game states**

- **Every peace breaks.**
  - The game opens on a cease-fire that "was supposed to" hold and did not (`4547658`, Kankaras).
  - The fall of Leboa-Sako brings "a cease-fire in the south" (`Objectives/A2SM06_APR_02`). The
    player's own false flag ends it: "Even this cease-fire was too much to ask for." (`4939226`,
    Doctor Obua).
  - Port Selao is a cease-fire zone; even there, restricted zones are shot on sight
    (`Tutorial/TU73_MESSAGE`).
- **The warlords' peace.** They want it to quiet the streets: "The people are too troubled. Too much
  unrest. What they need is order and security!" (`4561167`, Mbantuwe).
- **Peace is bad for business.**
  - The foreigners fear it: "It's a death sentence for independent operators like you and I."
    (`4898343`, Hakim Echebbi).
  - So do the lieutenants: "blokes like you and I are out of a job. Or worse" (`4559170`, Greaves).
- **The Jackal's verdict.** "They wanted privacy, is what they wanted… so they could go on with their
  raping and pillaging. - In peace, you see." (`4577695`, Jackal).
- **The King's peace.** He brought his gold "to broker a peace agreement. What he doesn't realize is
  that this is not possible." (`4558481`, Gakumba).

**Implied**

- In this war a cease-fire is a pause the strong use to reorganise, never an end. Everyone with
  power — warlords, lieutenants, mercenaries, the Jackal — has a reason to break each one. The
  player is the instrument that breaks them all.

## Civilians, refugees and the exodus

**The game states**

- **The civilians want out.**
  - "Everyone's trying to find a way out of the country. Only fools stay now." (`5160098`, taxi
    driver).
  - "people are desperate to get out of the country" (`5122787`, Underground operative).
  - "Many are trapped. Scared of what will happen if they are caught trying to leave." (`4921372`,
    Father Maliya).
- **The factions call them the enemy.** "THEY are the enemy" (`4971042`, Gakumba) and "THEY are the
  threat" (`4560022`, Tambossa). Both send troops into the villages: "They're rounding up the
  villagers as well." (`4911088`, Reuben).
- **They suffer in the camps.** "These pictures of the children in our camps, dying of dysentery."
  (`4559222`, Kouassi).
- **Only money gets anyone out.** "Only money and connections get you out now." (`5160475`, taxi
  driver); "Good money in passports. - Everybody wants to get the hell out of here!" (`4909162`,
  Xianyong Bai).
- **The exodus.** "He's going to evacuate this entire country. Every civilian… Everybody has their
  papers. They've all left their villages." (`4911306`, Reuben). "Try to imagine it. An entire
  country of refugees." (`5103766`, Reuben).
- **The outcome.** "over two million refugees were displaced to neighboring countries. Aid
  organizations noted a surprisingly small number of civilian casualties. This was credited to the
  life-saving efforts of the underground movement within the country." (`Coda/Para1`).

**Implied**

- The Underground's papers and the Jackal's exodus are one effort. The player carries passports for
  the Underground in both acts. The Jackal's exodus begins with "Everybody has their papers"
  (`4911306`), and the closing text credits the Underground with the low death toll. No string says
  the Jackal and the Underground worked together.

## Mercenaries and money

**The game states**

- **The player is one of many.** Mike's is full of "independent contractors" (`4972806`, Kankaras).
  Bastion UK, Macgrudder-Powell and "the older PMC guys" (`5105463`, Paul Ferenc) make up a trade
  with its own reputations.
- **Everyone assumes money is the motive.**
  - "since men like you only work for money, you're no longer my problem." (`4577361`, Jackal).
  - "You mercenaries are all the same. Like cheap whores." (`4889211`, Mbantuwe).
  - Michele Dachss, surprised: "So you DO do things for reasons other than money." (`4907338`,
    Michele Dachss).
- **The country eats them.**
  - "This place chews people up and spits them out" (`5107347`, Flora Guillen).
  - "Sometimes it feels like we bury our friends faster than we can make them here." (`5109719`,
    Nasreen Davar).
- **They get the blame.** "They're saying all the foreigners are to blame, and that they have to get
  rid of us. For the good of the country." (`4899395`, Andre Hyppolite).

**Implied**

- The buddies' arc mirrors the player's. They come for money, they are used by both factions, and in
  the end they cut a deal and turn on him. "I'm sorry, mate. Just business." (`5009973`, Frank
  Bilders).

## Disease as the war

**The game states**

The Jackal's image of the war is a disease:

- "the longer you stay, the deeper the virus goes. In the end, nobody will be left. Just the disease…
  Quarantine the patients." (`4577598`, Jackal).
- "It's a cancer… Every cell is infected." (`4577606`, Jackal).
- "We're going to cure these men of their disease." (`4577719`, Jackal).
- "We're have to isolate this disease, so we can destroy it." (`4920067`, Jackal).
- "Every cell of this cancer has to be destroyed. That includes you, AND me." (`4973416`, Jackal).

Others reach for the same image:

- Liberation Radio: "A plague has gripped our beautiful country and its name is A-P-R" (`5160431`,
  Liberation Radio?).
- The taxi driver on brush fires: "It spreads like a virus" (`5160156`, taxi driver).

**Implied**

- The image fits the plot. The Jackal's evacuation is the "quarantine", the civilians the people
  kept apart from the sick. The final killings — leaders, buddies, the two men themselves — are the
  cells that "have to be destroyed", the player included because he is infected too.

## Outsiders, aid and Africa

**The game states**

- **Both factions claim Africa for Africans.** "This is OUR struggle. Africa is for Africans."
  (`4558255`, Gakumba); "One Africa." (`4559819`, Tambossa).
- **Aid is captured.**
  - Kouassi: "it has to come to US first. We know how to handle it." (`4559227`, Kouassi).
  - Greaves: "None of it will ever get to those poor sods." (`4559231`, Greaves).
  - The APR's bomb instructor diverts air-dropped relief and sells it (`4934074`, Hakim Echebbi;
    `Objectives/A2LM12_03`).
- **Foreigners stand by.**
  - The Jackal: "people back home say they want to help… They wear their halos and they stand by,
    wringing their hands." (`4973442`, `4973477`, Jackal).
  - The closing text: "Reuben Oluwagembi's coverage of events has been ignored by the International
    Press." (`Coda/Para3`).
- **Foreign money pulls the strings.** Companies farm and pump the land (OGC, Mertens-Segolo,
  TaeMoCo). The King's money came "from Europe" (`4558425`, Carbonell), and his son's fortune sits
  "back in Belgium" (`Objectives/A1LM06_03`).
- **The last word.** Spoken as the plan to save the civilians ends in his own death: "Africa wins
  again." (`4972911`, Jackal).

**Implied**

- Every side uses outsiders and blames them:
  - The factions hire foreign mercenaries while preaching "Africa for Africans".
  - They take foreign aid while calling the foreigners spies.
  - Then they blame the foreigners for the assassinations they paid for.

## Information

**The game states**

- **Reuben's trade.** "Information is a weapon here. It's more useful than guns sometimes."
  (`4911492`, Reuben). "There is only one currency I can offer, and that is information."
  (`4911254`, Reuben).
- **Soldiers seize his tapes** (`4577477`, Reuben). The warlords want him out of the country
  (`4577502`, Reuben). The factions round up the journalists at the end (`4934582`, Reuben).
- **Propaganda.** Liberation Radio broadcasts lies on a censor's delay (`4910647`, DJ Lord Haw-Haw).
  "people believe what they hear. Especially on the radio." (`4934106`, Hakim Echebbi).

**Implied**

- The truth of this war reaches no one outside it. Reuben's tapes are taken, his story is ignored, and
  the Jackal's identity "remains a mystery" (`Coda/Para4`). What survives is what the player found
  and heard.
